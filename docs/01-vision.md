# 01 — Vision

## The philosophy

Improv has the idea of the **robot, the pirate and the ninja**:

- **The robot** brings order and follows the structure of the scene.
- **The pirate** brings chaos and breaks it.
- **The ninja** knows when to do which.

The ninja is not a lukewarm 50/50 blend. They commit fully to one side and know when to
switch. You become the ninja through the dance between order and chaos.

## The inspiration

A two-person show running 15 to 20 minutes. One actor read lines word for word from a real
play. The other, an improviser, had no idea what the script was. The scripted actor is the
ultimate robot: they cannot deviate, so the improviser cannot steer and can only
**justify**. The laughs come from watching the improviser justify lines that wreck their
theory of the scene.

## The idea

The audience creates the scene with AI. One person, the **host**, reads it. The
improvisers improvise against it.

## Core principles

- **Decided: the script is written once and never rewritten.** It is generated after the
  brainstorm and does not react to what the improvisers do. The script stays the robot,
  and the collision between script and improvisation is the comedy. There is no live
  generation during the scene.
- **Decided: the host and the improvisers discover the script at the same time.** The host
  reads cold, line by line. The improvisers discover it by hearing it.
- **Decided: the host's timing is the craft.** Dropping a line straight after an improviser
  commits makes a punchline; holding a line until the scene drifts makes a lifeline.
- **Decided: the audience can follow the full script.** They know what is coming and the
  improvisers don't, which turns the comedy into dramatic irony. This is where the name
  Foreshadow comes from.
- **Decided: write for the audience, trust the improvisers.** The AI's job is the best
  possible play the audience would want to see, through their votes. Nothing is written
  to help the improvisers; it is assumed nothing will go to plan, and holding the scene
  and the audience is their craft.
- **Decided: the audience must believe in the scene.** If they don't believe it is theirs
  and that it is real, the whole thing falls apart. See [04-brainstorm.md](04-brainstorm.md).
- **Decided: it doesn't have to be over the top.** Think of writing a scene from a play, or
  a sketch, not a whole play. See [05-scene-writing.md](05-scene-writing.md).
- **Decided: the design has to be supreme.** The screens exist to help the host and the
  audience do their jobs. See [03-roles-and-surfaces.md](03-roles-and-surfaces.md).
- **Decided: the host is the most essential role**, and the hardest and least appreciated.
  **Proposed:** when there is a trade-off, make the host's job easier.

## Naming

- **Foreshadow**, the app: it shows the audience what is coming before it happens.
- **Spoiler Alert**, the flagship format. Other formats could run on the app later.

## Out of scope for the MVP (decided)

Speech sync, improviser transcripts, live voting during the scene, user accounts for
anyone except the admin, and native apps.

## Later ideas (decided as future work)

- **Speech sync:** listen to the host's mic and move the script display along with what is
  being said, like synced lyrics, allowing manual override.
- **Improviser transcripts:** mic the improvisers and show their improvised lines between
  the scripted ones in a different colour, so the screen ends up holding the full scene
  that actually happened.
